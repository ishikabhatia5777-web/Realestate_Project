# Office Appraisal — Income & Sales Comparison Approaches

Covers Aura Estate's "office" category. Office property is usually valued primarily by the **Income Approach**, cross-checked against the **Sales Comparison Approach** where enough comparable transactions exist. (The same method extends to other commercial types — retail, warehouse/industrial — if they come up.)

## Income Approach (primary)

1. **Determine (or estimate) annual net operating income (NOI)**:
   - Gross rental income (current or market rent × leasable area)
   - Minus vacancy/collection loss (use a realistic vacancy rate for the local market, e.g. 5–10%, if not known)
   - Minus operating expenses (maintenance, property tax, insurance, management) — *excluding* debt service and depreciation

2. **Select a capitalization rate (cap rate)** appropriate for the property type, location, and tenant quality. Search for recent comparable cap rates in the local market if not supplied — cap rates vary significantly by city, asset class, and tenant credit quality, so don't assume a generic figure without checking.

3. **Value = NOI ÷ Cap Rate**

## Sales Comparison Approach (cross-check)

Same logic as residential but normalized to price per sq ft of leasable/built-up area, adjusted for:
- Location/footfall (especially for retail)
- Building grade/specifications (Grade A/B/C for offices)
- Lease structure and tenant quality (for income-producing assets)
- Age, condition, parking/loading access (especially for warehouses)

## Cost Approach (secondary, useful for unique/purpose-built assets)

Land value (via comparable land sales) + replacement/reproduction cost of the building − depreciation. Most useful when there's no active market of comparable income-producing sales (e.g. specialized industrial facilities).

## Reconciliation

Weight the Income Approach most heavily for leased/income-generating property; weight Sales Comparison more heavily for owner-occupied or vacant commercial space with good comparables. State which approach(es) you used and why.

## Notes

- Always state the cap rate and NOI assumptions explicitly — small changes in cap rate produce large value swings, so this is the most sensitive part of the estimate.
- If lease terms, tenant covenant strength, or upcoming lease expiries are known, flag how they affect risk and therefore value.
